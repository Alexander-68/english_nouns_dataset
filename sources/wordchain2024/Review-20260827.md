## Audit — nine findings

1. **[data] Source dictionary frozen at 2006.** NLTK ships Princeton WordNet 3.0 (`wn.get_version()` → `3.0`). A 68-word spot check of ordinary post-2006 vocabulary found 28 missing: smartphone, selfie, podcast, emoji, hashtag, chatbot, malware, bitcoin, blockchain, influencer, wifi, app, vape, quinoa, pilates, hoodie, earbud, heatwave, microplastic, telehealth, …
2. **[method] "Contains a capital letter" is the wrong proper-noun test.** Wrong both ways: it files non-name acronyms (`pH`, `mRNA`, `AARP`) as names, and leaves 693 genuine proper nouns in the noun list (amazon, andromeda, atlas, babel, bacon, balboa, baedeker, anjou, astrakhan…). WordNet marks these structurally via `instance_hypernyms()`.
3. **[data] Plural contamination is systematic.** The notebook hand-excludes two words (`shorts`, `pants`). Actual scale: **452** entries ending in *-s* whose own singular is also in the list — acoustics, aesthetics, affairs, arms, arts, assets, blues, bowels, bleachers, belongings, braces, auspices, antipodes…
4. **[data] British/American spellings counted as separate nouns.** 215 `-isation`/`-ization` pairs, 43 `-our`/`-or` pairs. One word, two skins — and any future frequency ranking splits their counts.
5. **[data] The CSV loses two words on every read.** Pandas parses `nan` and `null` as missing. Both are real nouns in the list. Use `pd.read_csv(..., keep_default_na=False, na_values=[])`.
6. **[method] The spaCy NER experiment could not have worked.** NER infers entity type from sentence context; a comma-joined dump of 41,466 context-free nouns supplies none. That is why `vagina` and `lycoperdon` came back GPE. The input was invalid, not the model. Same for the scispaCy run that returned zero entities.
7. **[method] The postponed classification was already inside WordNet.** Every noun synset carries a hand-assigned 26-way supersense (`lexname()`) — free, deterministic, no model needed. Distribution over the shipped list: person 6,130 · artifact 5,998 · act 3,665 · state 2,938 · attribute 2,804 · communication 2,765 · animal 2,424 · plant 2,349 · substance 2,227 · cognition 1,777 · food 1,075 · body 1,045 · quantity 852 · group 792 · event 746 · process 623 · object 552 · location 503 · feeling 498 · possession 417 · time 396 · phenomenon 318 · relation 251 · shape 247 · motive 41 · Tops 33.
8. **[method] Wikipedia measures notability, not wordhood.** *aardvark* has an article; *rarity*, *clemency*, *jauntiness* do not — and they are better chain words. As a filter it would delete common abstract nouns and keep taxonomic Latin. Keep summaries as game flavour text only.
9. **[scope] Discarding compounds dropped 46% of the corpus, undocumented.** 35,247 underscore + 3,189 hyphen entries. That is a rules choice (many traditions allow *ice cream → milk*), invisible to anyone receiving only the CSV, and it swept out single-token compounds like `soul_mate`. Keep discards as a labelled column, not a deletion.

**Y is the kill move.** 3,619 nouns end in *y*; 119 begin with it, and only 46 of those have a Wikipedia article. The human-reachable counter-supply is roughly forty words (yam, yak, yard, yarn, year, yeast, yellow, yen, yew, yoga, yogurt, yolk, youth…). Conversely *j*, *v*, *q* are the safest endings and the worst things to be handed.

Proposed new columns: `start_supply`, `end_demand`, `trap_score`.

## Sources that appeared while the project slept

- **Open English WordNet 2025** (released 2024-12-31, annual) — living successor to WN 3.0; GWA XML / RDF / JSON / WNDB. The 2025 edition **moved proper nouns into a separate Open English Namenet**, plus a "2025+" build with curated proper nouns retained. Replaces the capital-letter regex outright. https://github.com/globalwordnet/english-wordnet
- **`wn` Python library v1.0.0** (Feb 2026) — modern replacement for `nltk.corpus.wordnet`; loads any WordNet edition into local SQLite. https://pypi.org/project/wn/
- **Wiktextract / kaikki.org** — English extract dated 2026-08-05: 964,187 noun senses + 274,572 proper-name senses as JSONL, with countable/uncountable/plural-only tags, regional labels and inflected forms. Fixes findings 1, 3, 4, 9 structurally. Licence: CC BY-SA — share-alike propagates downstream. https://kaikki.org/dictionary/English/
- **Kuperman AoA norms** (30,000 words) + **Brysbaert concreteness** (40,000 lemmas) — human-rated age of acquisition. This is the empirical answer to the 05Y/10Y/50Y tiers, citable and reproducible where an LLM's judgement is not. Kuperman, Stadthagen-González & Brysbaert, *Behavior Research Methods* 44 (2012).
- **SUBTLEX-US** — subtitle frequency; predicts what people reach for under a 10-second timer far better than book or web frequency.
- **wordfreq** — sunset Sept 2024 (author cites AI-polluted web text). The frozen snapshot is arguably *better* here: it captures pre-contamination human usage. Pin it deliberately and document that.
- **LisanBench** — an existing public LLM word-chain benchmark (one-letter-edit ladders), scoring rule-following, vocabulary, planning, recall, persistence. Prior art for WCGT-AI. Last-letter + category-constrained chaining is still unoccupied, but the docx claim that "a kid can easily win" was written against GPT-4-1106 and must be re-tested against 2026 reasoning models before publication. https://lisanbench.com/

## Resumption plan

1. Revoke the Wikimedia credentials; clean the docx.
2. Re-extract from OEWN 2025 via `wn`. Keep the old CSV as `v0` — the WN3.0 → OEWN2025 delta is itself publishable.
3. Join against Wiktextract on lemma: countability, plural-only flags, spelling variants, modern entries. Keep every dropped entry as a labelled row, never a deletion.
4. Attach WordNet supersenses, then Kuperman AoA, then SUBTLEX frequency. Reserve the LLM classifier for the residue — and grade it against the human norms so its accuracy can be stated.
5. Compute the chain graph: 26×26 transition matrix, trap scores, dead-end probabilities. Open question worth a paper: **what is the longest possible Word Chain?** (longest-trail problem on a directed multigraph).
6. Ship as a versioned open dataset — Hugging Face or Zenodo with a DOI, a dataset card documenting every filter as a reversible flag, and a share-alike-compatible licence.
7. Then WCGT-AI. The benchmark needs the referee; the referee needs the dataset. That dependency is why it stalled.

## Adjacent ideas

- **Trap-score game mode** — difficulty becomes a dial, not a word list. Easy mode bans *-y* endings; hard mode rewards them.
- **Phonetic Word Chain** — chain on final *sound* via the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Closer to spoken play, and it rewrites the letter economy (*-y* stops being a trap). A second edge set over the same nouns, nearly free.
- **The 20-year diff as a paper** — identical pipeline over WN 3.0 and OEWN 2025; publish what English gained and lost. Few people have the 2006-vintage extraction already on disk in documented form.

## Strategic note

The dataset's value was never its size. Anyone can dump 41,466 nouns. What nobody has published is a noun list that knows how hard each word is, how often people say it, and what it does to a chain.
